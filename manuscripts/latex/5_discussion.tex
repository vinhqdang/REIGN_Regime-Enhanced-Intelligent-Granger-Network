\section{Discussion}

The substantial performance gains observed in REIGN can be attributed to two major architectural decisions. First, by implementing PELT regime detection, the neural engine is shielded from attempting to map conflicting causal relationships that drift over time, an issue that actively degrades global algorithms like standard CUTS+ or PC. Second, bypassing the LASSO coarse-discovery phase allows the Graph Neural Network to maintain full expressive capacity over the dense adjacency matrix.

While the current experiments showcase REIGN's autonomous learning capabilities (achieving state-of-the-art without a strong prior), the framework's true potential lies in its Zero-Shot LLM integration. In industry applications, Large Language Models can reliably deduce macroeconomic causal directions (e.g., Interest Rates $\rightarrow$ Inflation). By increasing the $\lambda_{prior}$ hyperparameter, researchers can anchor the neural engine's loss landscape, fundamentally solving the sample-inefficiency problem prevalent in deep causal discovery.

However, REIGN is not without limitations. The continuous optimization of the augmented Lagrangian constraint requires significantly more computational overhead per regime than constraint-based methods. Future work will investigate low-rank approximations of the GNN message-passing layers to accelerate convergence on datasets with hundreds of nodes.
