\section{Introduction}

Causal discovery in multivariate time series is a fundamental problem across various domains, ranging from neuroscience to macroeconomics. The goal is to infer the underlying directed acyclic graph (DAG) representing cause-and-effect relationships from purely observational data. Traditional approaches, such as constraint-based methods (e.g., PC algorithm) and score-based methods, assume that the underlying data-generating process is stationary. However, in highly dynamic environments like financial markets, the statistical properties of the system undergo sudden, unpredictable shifts due to macroeconomic events, policy changes, or market shocks. These structural breaks violate the stationarity assumption, leading to spurious correlations and catastrophic degradation in the performance of classical causal discovery algorithms.

Recent advancements have attempted to bridge this gap by extending structural equation models (SEMs) into the temporal domain. Methods like PCMCI+ \cite{runge2019detecting} and DYNOTEARS \cite{pamfil2020dynotears} excel at capturing lagged dependencies but struggle when confronted with hidden nonstationary regimes. Concurrently, deep learning approaches have introduced neural Granger causality using Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), such as CUTS+ \cite{cheng2023cuts}, which handles high-dimensional and irregular time series but remains agnostic to both sudden regime shifts and domain-specific topological knowledge.

To address these limitations, we propose the \textbf{Regime-Enhanced Intelligent Granger Network (REIGN)}, an end-to-end causal discovery framework specifically designed for nonstationary environments. REIGN tackles the nonstationarity challenge by integrating statistical changepoint detection directly into the causal pipeline, segmenting the time series into locally stationary regimes. To further constrain the vast search space of high-dimensional DAGs, REIGN introduces an innovative prior-injection mechanism that leverages Large Language Models (LLMs) to automatically synthesize domain-specific structural priors. Finally, a coarse-to-fine neural Granger engine processes each regime, optimizing a continuous adjacency matrix subject to an augmented Lagrangian DAG constraint. 

The primary contributions of this work are three-fold:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We introduce a novel architecture that isolates nonstationary temporal dynamics into locally stationary regimes using the Pruned Exact Linear Time (PELT) algorithm, enabling robust, regime-specific causal inference.
    \item We pioneer the integration of LLM-generated structural priors into a continuous optimization framework, dynamically calibrating the neural network's initial search space without requiring human expert annotation.
    \item We demonstrate through rigorous empirical evaluation on synthetic nonstationary Vector Autoregressive (VAR) datasets that REIGN achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming established baselines such as PCMCI+ and PC by over 11\% in F1 score.
\end{enumerate}
