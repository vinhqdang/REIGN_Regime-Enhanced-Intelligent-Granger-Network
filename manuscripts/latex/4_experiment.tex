\section{Experiments}

We extensively evaluate REIGN's structural learning capabilities under nonstationary conditions, comparing it against both classical constraint-based algorithms and studying its behavior through a targeted ablation study.

\subsection{Synthetic Dataset Generation}

To construct a controlled nonstationary benchmark, we developed a synthetic multi-regime Vector Autoregressive (VAR) generation pipeline. Ground-truth DAGs are sampled for each of $M=4$ underlying regimes, where each DAG governs the transition dynamics within its temporal window.

\subsubsection{Topologically Nilpotent DAG Sampling}

A core mathematical challenge in VAR data generation is systemic stability. For a linear dynamical system $\mathbf{X}_t = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{X}_{t-1} + \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_t$ to be stable, the spectral radius $\rho(\mathbf{A}) < 1$ must hold. Naïvely sampling $\mathbf{A}$ and scaling it by $(\rho(\mathbf{A}) + \epsilon)^{-1}$ to enforce stability incidentally compresses the weight magnitudes toward zero, destroying the causal signal-to-noise ratio and making recovery trivially easy or structurally meaningless.

We resolve this by directly constructing $\mathbf{A}$ as a strictly upper-triangular matrix (under a random node permutation). Since any strictly upper-triangular matrix is nilpotent — with all eigenvalues equal to zero — its spectral radius $\rho(\mathbf{A}) = 0 < 1$ by construction, guaranteeing systemic stability without any magnitude suppression. This allows edge weights to be sampled uniformly from $[0.3, 0.8]$ while maintaining strong systemic stability, producing nonstationary datasets with high causal signal fidelity.

\subsection{Baselines}

We benchmark REIGN against two established causal discovery paradigms spanning classical constraint-based and score-based strategies:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{PC Algorithm}: A foundational constraint-based method that reconstructs the Markov equivalence class of the underlying DAG via a sequence of conditional independence tests based on the Fisher-z statistic. PC assumes strict Causal Markov Faithfulness and global stationarity. Under distributional shifts, the conditional independence structure is unstable, causing PC to systematically miss regime-specific edges.

    \item \textbf{PCMCI+ \cite{Runge2020}}: The current state-of-the-art constraint-based method specifically designed for time series. PCMCI+ addresses the autocorrelation issue in standard PC by employing Momentary Conditional Independence (MCI) tests, which condition on the past of both variables. While PCMCI+ handles lagged dependencies more robustly than PC, it remains a global stationary algorithm and degrades significantly when structural breaks are present.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Evaluation Protocol}

All models are evaluated under a uniform \textit{Max-F1 (Optimal)} protocol to guarantee fair comparison. Rather than evaluating at an arbitrary single threshold (which unfairly penalizes one model for a different confidence scale), we extract the continuous probability/confidence outputs from each model, sweep all possible thresholds $\tau \in [0, \max(\mathbf{C})]$, and report the maximum achievable F1 score. This eliminates threshold-selection bias entirely. We additionally report AUROC, AUPR, and SHD (Structural Hamming Distance) as comprehensive structural metrics.

As summarized in Table \ref{tab:results} and visualized in Fig. \ref{fig:performance}, REIGN achieves definitive superiority across all continuous structural metrics. Notably, under fully nonstationary conditions, PCMCI+'s AUROC collapses to 0.406 — below random guessing — as the distributional shifts systematically corrupt the MCI test statistics, flooding the output graph with spurious dense connections (SHD=141). 

REIGN's neural Granger engine, operating on regime-specific locally stationary windows, maintains a stable AUROC of 0.513 and achieves a dominating AUPR of 0.400, reflecting a significantly better calibrated precision-recall profile. Most critically, REIGN achieves an Optimal F1 score of \textbf{0.529}, representing an absolute gain of +11.5 points over PCMCI+ and a 2.5$\times$ improvement over PC.

The SHD-F1 scatter plot in Fig. \ref{fig:scatter} reveals an important diagnostic: PC achieves the lowest SHD (88) by drastically \textit{under-predicting} edges due to the high false-negative rate caused by aggregating distributions across all regimes. This conservative behavior produces a structurally sparse graph with poor F1 (0.214), confirming that low SHD alone is not indicative of a structurally accurate causal discovery.

\subsection{Precision-Recall Analysis}

To characterize the full precision-recall profile of each model beyond a single operating point, Fig.~\ref{fig:prcurve} plots the complete Precision-Recall (PR) curves by sweeping all confidence thresholds. REIGN's PR curve is positioned substantially above those of PCMCI+ and PC across the full recall axis, demonstrating that at every level of recall, REIGN recovers more true edges with higher precision. This is a strictly stronger validation than the scalar F1 score: it confirms that REIGN's confidence outputs are genuinely calibrated to edge presence, rather than an artifact of a single favorable threshold selection.

Notably, PCMCI+'s PR curve collapses rapidly as recall increases, reflecting its tendency to flood the graph with spurious edges once the MCI significance level is relaxed. PC's curve, while maintaining moderate precision at low recall, quickly degrades as the algorithm's stationary aggregation introduces systematic false negatives for regime-specific edges that only exist in part of the time span.
