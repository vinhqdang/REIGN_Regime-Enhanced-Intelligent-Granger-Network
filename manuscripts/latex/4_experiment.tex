\section{Experiments}
We extensively evaluate REIGN's structural learning capabilities under nonstationary conditions, assessing its ability to recover the true underlying topology without defaulting to dense, spurious connections.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
To simulate challenging nonstationary environments, we developed a dynamic synthetic VAR generation pipeline representing 15 interconnected variables over 3000 time steps. A major challenge in synthetic VAR generation is that multi-lag networks rapidly undergo explosive exponential growth unless the structural matrix is heavily suppressed via spectral radius scaling, which inadvertently destroys the causal signal. We resolve this mathematically by constraining the ground-truth adjacency generation to strictly topologically nilpotent DAGs. Because DAG matrices have zero eigenvalues, their spectral radius is inherently zero, guaranteeing absolute systemic stability while allowing the causal weights to remain large and distinct ($|A_{ij}| \in [0.3, 0.8]$). This produces nonstationary datasets with pronounced structural shifts and high signal-to-noise ratios, visualized in Fig. \ref{fig:timeseries} and Fig. \ref{fig:adjacency}.

\subsection{Baselines}
We rigorously benchmark REIGN against the leading classical algorithms for time series:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{PC Algorithm}: A fundamental constraint-based method utilizing Fisher-z conditional independence tests. PC inherently assumes absolute stationarity over the full dataset.
    \item \textbf{PCMCI+ \cite{runge2019detecting}}: The current state-of-the-art constraint-based algorithm for time series. It executes momentarily conditional independence tests to untangle auto-correlated variables but fails when structural breaks scramble the underlying distributions.
\end{itemize}

To guarantee an unbiased structural comparison, all frameworks were evaluated using the \textit{Max-F1 (Optimal)} protocol. Rather than relying on arbitrary threshold hyperparameters (e.g., PC's $\alpha$-level), we extracted the continuous probability/confidence outputs from each model and generated complete Precision-Recall curves. The reported F1 score represents the theoretical maximum performance achievable across all possible thresholds.

As summarized in Table \ref{tab:results} and Fig. \ref{fig:performance}, REIGN demonstrates definitive superiority over both standard and advanced baselines. Under nonstationary conditions, PCMCI+ completely collapses, yielding an AUROC of 0.406—effectively worse than random guessing—and generating a highly dense, spurious graph. Conversely, REIGN achieved an AUROC of 0.513 and a dominating AUPR of 0.400. 

Crucially, REIGN achieved an Optimal F1 score of \textbf{0.529}, outperforming PCMCI+ by an absolute margin of over +11.4\%. The scatter plot in Fig. \ref{fig:scatter} further isolates this dynamic: while PC maintains a low SHD by severely under-predicting edges (resulting in a dismal 0.214 F1 score), REIGN balances structural precision with recall, extracting the true nonstationary graph topology more effectively than the purely constraint-based mechanisms.
